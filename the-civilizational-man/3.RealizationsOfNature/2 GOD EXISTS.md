GOD EXISTS

The realization is that God is in charge and you have little say. You pray to God to win the lotto and he’s like “nah bruh. Lol.” You are in a big play so vastly large that it is impossible for us to comprehend the boundaries, let alone the detail in front of our faces.
I didn’t accept God into my life until I was in my late 30s. Despite having obvious signs and mystical experiences, I never attributed it to a deity. In my 20s I was a moderate Leftist and Occupy Wall Street organizer, but it wasn’t until I had my children that my ideology began to change. The first domino to fall was that when I examined my philosophies I found many were not based in history or perennialism, and they had an anti-civilizational bent.

I can’t say there was any particular thing that I can point to that compelled me to convert to a believer, but a series of revelations and realizations about society, humanity, and civilization that slowly chipped away at my core. And I don’t regret my belief in the slightest. In fact, I am a happier person having done so, and I feel as though I have joined humanity, rather than going against the grain and becoming the Ubermensch.

Having gone through my transformation on God, society, and everything in between, I do feel that Leftist ideologies are trying to strip the attributes of humanity out of the human animal. God welcomes all and accepts you as is, rather than trying to be something other than you are.
For those that are unaware, my idea of God is not within the traditional lens of Christianity, Judaism, or Islam. In my first book, Novum Carta, I lay out a religion and religious philosophy. I look at the underlying nature of religion, build, and synthesize different parts of many different religions into one. This may be blasphemy to some hard-line religious folks, but where others see division, I see a thread of humanity that combines us rather than divides us. I believe that those tribes of different beliefs but walking in similar paths, with shared goals, and of mutual pro-civilizational policies are allies.

It is my belief that religion didn’t come from civilization, but rather religion created civilization. Belief is a hard thing for those lacking it to understand. It is a drawing force and a force of missionaries. Spiritual experiences have always existed, and those coalesced into belief. Belief created temples and people congregated to those temples. They set up homes to be closer to God(s). To help the temple they created specialties. A congregation becomes a town, and a town becomes a city. Sumer sprouted because of religion, not the other way around.

This perennial notion is the driving force of civilization. To deny yourself a key part of the past and your genetic lineage is anti-civilizational and creates a gap in the psyche that other things fill. Like politics, ideology, drugs, and evil.
