ON THE MACHINE

Curtis Yarvin coined the term "The Cathedral" to give a concept of the alliance between the State, the media, and the education system. In this alliance there is an alliance and agreement between these entities to perpetuate the State and the ruling faction in dictating society's future. This is done through the acts of people, agreements, incentives, and consensus. These acts that cause this formulation are what I call "The Machine," and it is more far-reaching than just the three elements previously mentioned.

The Machine is broader in that it incorporates corporations into the fray.

We have witnessed a Catch-22 in the time that I exist in that the governments of the West sanctioned the importation of tens of millions of migrants from the third world into their societies. They did this under the guise of compassion while at the same time providing a future tax base, because the local native populations were not breeding; no people means government shrinks, but that is also true for corporations. Modernity revolves around money and an infinite growth mentality. If growth isn't achieved, then several societal effects happen, up to and including collapse.

However, the governments of the West didn't give their populations a referendum to vote on this societal change. Instead they propagandized to the mentally ill and simpletons by injecting mind viruses into The Cathedral and online. This gave them the power of the "governed" to then enact those policies. This in my opinion broke the social contract between the people and those in power. However, there is no grievance chain, and those in my time know all too well there is no accountability, which leaves only a few courses of action by the native population.

The Machine is the utilization of institutions and corporations through policies and direction of government to turn civil society into a self-replicating commodity to create commodities. In other words, you are a cog that makes cogs to ensure that the system doesn't fall apart. The Machine is the linkage of the cogs, the fuel, the input, the output; the flow of energy from one system to another to produce a result; the utilization of systems to enforce itself or others.

When there are not enough cogs or energy in a system, it cannot grow. Growth is something we can somewhat reliably predict. System failure is something harder to determine, because energy flow from one system to another may compromise another, because the required energy to sustain itself or another system is less; the dominoes fall. I go deep into this topic in Novum Carta in a chapter called the Limits of Complexity, but I will spare the chapter and just give the premise. The premise is that as the entropy of a system increases, so does its propensity for failure. The more complex a system is, the more that can go wrong with it. The more that can go wrong, the harder it is to maintain and fix. The more complex a system is, the harder it is to keep it going and improving. There will be a time when systems are so complex that they collapse from within.

The Machine is a vast system of individual cogs, systems, sub-systems, and entities with names of businesses and countries interacting with other cogs, systems, sub-systems, and entities. We give the working of systems in The Machine names like sociology, politics, economics, and geopolitics, to name a few. But they are all interconnected, and when the energy to sustain the system dwindles the systems react: like importing millions of cogs to support the system. Here we find the union of the Leftist and the Civic Nationalist. The problem arises when the cogs consume and don't produce; here we find the Nationalist.

Where political philosophy at the far ends of the spectrum fails, and why utopia is unachievable, is because you must identify all components of The Machine. You must change the fuel source of the majority of the individual cogs on up, and somehow cope and plan for the external systems linking resources, fuel, and survivability on a geo-political level. It is conceivable that an ideology could be deliberately constructed so that its adoption becomes compulsory in the way technological adoption is. That aside, we must examine The Machine in the context of a civilizational asset or an obstacle.

Because governments and institutions, and the people who run them, can be co-opted, they can be pro- and anti-civilizational. When you have people who put others into power who adhere to death-cult-like ideologies and beliefs, or progressives who base policies on feelings rather than grounded rational understanding of the human animal, The Machine becomes a system of oppression of the individual cog. The cog must then determine if participating in The Machine is worth it, and this is where factions of cogs will then tear down The Machine, or create decentralized Machines. 

The theme of Leftism is revolution and tearing down structures. It is their nature, like a lion feeding on a gazelle, half-baked into their genes while their ideologies compel them to the fact. But they will change The Machine to fit their goals. While on the Right you have people who build The Machine, create the guardrails, and hold the individual cogs as the centerpiece rather than the institutional systems. It is, however, not always that simple, but it is memetic.

The Machine will always exist and has always existed in one design or another. It is important for the civilizational man to know this fact, for if culture and policy downstream determine the fuel source for The Machine, then adequate attention must be paid to it.
