FIRST PILLAR

We must acknowledge that to be alive is to suffer discontent. Even if it is formed within the mind or formed externally, life is a sequence of suffering events and the accomplishments over them. Even Buddhist monks in monasteries must overcome suffering. Even the person living in the woods, away from civilization, in sustenance, must overcome suffering of the environment and of the mind. For to exist is to suffer.

Utopian idealism promises that once X happens, then suffering will cease, but even in our dreams we suffer. Even with an abundance of food, a million-dollar mansion, adequate sex, abundant socialization, enough intoxicants at night, we suffer. To be numb to the world through morphine on our deathbed, without much of a care in the world, do we suffer. The eradication of inequality, in perfect equity, do we strive for something else. 
That is the Buddhist teaching. For every mile we give. For every achievement. The monkey mind wonders what can be done better, faster, and what comes next. We are never content. We never surrender to the basic needs. We must achieve more. More. More. For that is our programming. That is our existence, and for millennia man, in one shape or another, has contended with this longing.

It is the hardest realization for a Buddhist and the hardest to enact. In that too is utopian idealism urging us to become more than we are. But WE accept that we yearn for a better X. We yearn for a better tomorrow in the suffering we exist within. As such, we accept that life is suffering.
In the movie The Matrix, the first iteration of the program failed because the machines built a Utopia. Those humans inside of it rejected it at a biological and rational level because humans paradoxically need and want to avoid suffering. A life without struggle is artificial and people will create struggle when none exists; this is the notion from Dostoevsky.

We exist to suffer and there is nothing wrong with that. It has created beautiful music, poems of renown, art lasting generations, and a drive for the human animal to always improve, to never stagnate, and to walk through the fire.
