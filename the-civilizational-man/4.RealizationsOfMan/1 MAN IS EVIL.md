MAN IS EVIL

Man is born to murder but is guided by the grace of God, temperance, morality, and self-discipline. He has a right to pursue uninterrupted commerce, education, and communication; however, man does not have the right to life. His life can be forfeit based on the laws he breaks. Because man is evil, society must have unwavering laws involving the destruction of like-life; murder forfeits your life, attempted murder forfeits your life, mass accidental manslaughter forfeits your life, manufacturing drugs or pharmaceuticals from individuals or companies that cause direct death forfeits life.

The only caveat and gray area of morality here is in weapons manufacturing. Because in such a realization, all men and women, for that matter, hold evil within them. Every man and woman, by law, must carry with them personal protection against others – I am advocating that all people of appropriate age carry on their person a gun. Castle doctrine, a law that states if you are assaulted with intent to harm, you have the legal right to hold your ground and defend yourself or others, must be enacted without impunity. Justified murder is still murder, and social consequences must be enacted. Social consequences must be enacted for all crimes of high degree.

James Madison once wrote in Federalist No. 51, “If men were angels, there would be no need for government.” The books The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene and Joost Elffers, and The Laws of Human Nature by Robert Greene were created to define how humans interact with one another. Man is corruptible, fallen from grace, selfish, ambitious; power corrupts, and the answer is to build structures that constrain imperfect people.

So man builds laws, but laws are only abided by good people. This is to say that good people are hindered by laws, which is why it is imperative to design a society where good people strive, and bad people are hindered. If we look at our two choices of totalitarianism and absolute freedom, there must be a middle ground. In totalitarianism, everything is set in stone, whereas in absolute freedom, it is chaos. For this reason I reject libertarianism as utopian, though it is less evil than totalitarianism. Libertarianism has been debated for decades, but enacting it requires a level of internal cultural symbiosis that is unattainable and externally requires that countries on the national borders are honorable. 

I reject totalitarianism because all totalitarian systems violate the individual. Those structures are, in many cases, utopian as well, where the population is propagandized to believe that they are the Ubermensch, that their country is the greatest, the people the best on the planet. So, between collectivism and the atomized individual, where can we find the least evil? 

The concept I want to pass on, that we will explore later, is Ordered Liberty. In such a system, it flirts with authoritarianism but isn't consumed by it. But even in such a system, evil persists. For if we kill a killer, is that not evil from the perspective of the killer's family or the killer himself? If we create policy to protect our society, is that not evil to the outsider? In all perspectives, there is evil, but the "third rail" that must be brought into the equation is "why" such an action is being performed. From this, all morality and ethics come.

If we deport thirty million non-citizens from the United States that are here on visas for information technology jobs, is that an evil act? Is freeing those job positions and giving them to people who were born here a just act? Is a country's allegiance to its people or ALL people in the world? If men revoked women's right to vote in Western civilization, is this an evil act? Even if it raised our collapsing birthrates, children were reared as they once were, families were closer, and reversed the slow march to civil war? Perspective matters because not all morality is the same. That is why when people say that the "culture wars" mean nothing, they aren't grasping that those who hold cultural control also control the morality of a nation.

Evil, then, is perspective but also res ipsa loquitur (it speaks for itself). If you lined up thirty million non-citizens and executed them, that is an evil that speaks for itself, whereas sending those to another country to live is not. In the society I live in today, res ipsa loquitur is confused, mostly by those on the Left. My job forcing me to take the Covid-19 shot that gave me myocarditis, where I almost died, is a form of evil from my perspective, but to those health and government professionals it wasn't. The argument is whether the evil was res ipsa loquitur, which I believe society has diverted from, and which is a litmus test we must use for ethics and morality.

Sages, prophets, and philosophers have identified for millennia that suffering is universal in that to live is to suffer. It is hard to hold nature responsible on the same footing as the suffering created by man. A hurricane creates mass destruction, wherein you can blame God and ascribe a divine purpose. But the destruction created by man FEELS more individualized and targeted. When you are mugged, you don't ascribe a greater meaning to it. When someone starts a forest fire that burns homes and displaces thousands, it was them serving the chaos gods or living as mentally ill; there was no purpose but to create suffering. A storm is just a storm, but a man can be evil for the purpose of being evil and self-serving.

It is also hard to not ascribe malice to the ignorance and arrogance of man, where even the best intentions can cause evil. I talk so much in this book about the chaos caused by psychopathy and sociopathy, as if it was the central theme. But these people are the true agents of evil, because in Schopenhauer's metaphysical and ethical framework of "compassion" and "oneness" they see themselves as the main character and others as a means rather than the representation of the one, thus promoting the suffering of the world.

This isn't just an individual endeavor, but one of mass movements. Think of a government instilling propaganda into another country to destabilize it through revolution or instilling mental illness through various means with mind-viruses. It is all-encompassing from the individual on up, and so the fact that man is evil must be known to the civilizational man.
