WE CAN NOT TELL FRIEND FROM FOE

“For every manifest case of insanity there are, in my estimation, at least ten latent cases who seldom get to the point of breaking out openly but whose views and behavior, for all their appearance of normality, are influenced by unconsciously morbid and perverse factors” – Carl Jung, The Undiscovered Self.

Because we cannot readily tell who is evil and who is normal, those that are evil must be readily identified. For any crime committed and justly convicted, the criminal is tattooed with a symbol or symbols depicting such crime on their face. In cases of high crimes involving bodily injury or death of another and the victims immediate family, siblings, and parents are likewise tattooed on their hands signifying that their genes have the propensity for evil and violence.

This is done purposely, and in doing so we are creating an “othering of people” of society, which happens naturally. Groups of people become tribes through ideas, hobbies, politics, regional location, sports teams, and form caste systems regardless of government intervention. Here, we are forming an out-group of literal criminals and agents of chaos versus those who honor order and law.

In the Pandemic, I was homeless and jobless for around a year. For 71 days I lived in the woods of Oklahoma enduring a primitive life in the blistering heat of Summer; taking water bottle showers, resting in the afternoons in the shade, filtering and purifying river water, hunting deer in the morning, and understanding the relationship of man in the wild.

One day I came across the largest rattlesnake I had ever seen. The type of snake you’d see in a zoo enclosure and think that it needed to be moved to a larger exhibit. This snake was sleeping under the shade of a tree. I retrieved my shotgun, took aim, and ended its life. In that moment I drew an interesting parallel with humanity.

A snake is just a snake. Its only concern is to eat, mate, and defend itself. Humanity has that combination but infinitely more rationales and needs to fulfill. A snake will strike when provoked, but a human will kill you because it is bored. A snake doesn’t invade your home to steal your TV. A snake doesn’t breed with you for financial and social gain. A snake doesn’t destroy your livelihood for profits. It doesn’t create laws or enforce them outside the natural laws. The reasons of humans are infinite, but for the snake as a predator, its reasons and justifications for its actions are finite. For that reason, we cannot tell friend from foe in human form.
