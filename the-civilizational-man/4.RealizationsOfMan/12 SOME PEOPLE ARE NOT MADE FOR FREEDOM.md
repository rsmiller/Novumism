SOME PEOPLE ARE NOT MADE FOR FREEDOM

In Dostoevsky’s The Brothers Karamazov, Christ returns to earth during the Spanish Inquisition and is arrested. The aged Grand Inquisitor visits his cell and delivers a long accusation. His central charge is that Christ made a catastrophic mistake in loving humanity too much by giving people freedom.

Christ wanted people to follow him freely, out of love, not because he'd filled their bellies. But the Inquisitor insists most people are too weak for that. Freedom, in his telling, is an unbearable burden. He says there's nothing more agonizing for a person than the search for someone to hand that freedom to. So people will bring their freedom to whoever feeds them and say, in effect, enslave us but feed us. The banner raised against Christ reads: feed men first, then ask virtue of them.

In recent memory we see this similarity where freedom was removed in the West under the guise of protection after the 9/11 attacks and the 7/7 attacks in Britain. We saw an erasure of an old world and the ushering in of police states. During those times the population gave consent and the governments seized. The humor is that only ten years later those governments ushered in the largest migration in history from those countries, of the religion, and of the factions who performed those attacks. So, not only did we get our freedom reduced, but we also have to contend with future sectarian violence.

There is a type of person who views freedom as a weight, a weight of infinite possibilities, anxiety of choice, or lawlessness. This large percentage of people will trade that freedom for bread, false hope, or power. That trade doesn’t even require force; they give it willingly. They will embrace slavery for full bellies and to have their decisions made for them.

Totalitarianism doesn’t grow from successful societies; it manifests as a symptom of a society that is both psychologically and spiritually unwell. It climbs to prominence through force while at the same time asking for permission from those who will trade freedom for bread. Carl Jung mapped this totalitarian rise and pinned it on the individual’s and society's decay. His five warnings are:
    1. Reason works until people get scared
    2. The most dangerous people look normal
    3. You don’t know yourself so tyrants will exploit this
    4. If you lose God, the State becomes your God
    5. The only defense is the individual the crowd can’t dissolve
