HUMANS TEND FOR HIERARCHY

We have predominantly existed as small tribes for 95% of human history, as far as we know. Only a handful of times in this time frame have cities flourished in populations of a million residents or slightly more. In all of these cases, hierarchies of status in many ways form, and the reasons for these hierarchies are multifold.

In small tribes of people we often find the wise archetype, where we follow a person because they have lived experience. You will also find the warrior archetype where societies are in constant conflict with one another, the environment has predators, or you must hunt to survive. Then you will find the religious archetype where hierarchy forms around a religion. Then there are the blends of the aforementioned: the oracle, the king, the shaman, the elder, and more.

The reasons these formed and still exist today are because they create purpose, meaning, accountability, trust, cohesion, and protection. Entire books have been written about each of these, but put simply, hierarchy allows us to fulfill our individual requirements per Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs: physiological, safety, belongingness, esteem, and self-actualization.

The needs of society shift as things get better or worse. For instance, calm political hierarchy is only afforded to stable non-migratory societies. Nomadic societies forced into migrations through war or natural events are usually dogmatic, ruthless, and autocratic. In a chaotic world where potentially millions of people will be migrating in a few short years, those political institutions will most likely not stand the test of time.

Put yourself in a village on the coast of Europe in a tribe of humans that lived 12,000 years ago. Over the course of a year, the sea levels could have risen forty feet or more, displacing you miles inland. The tribal hierarchy could have survived, but could political hierarchy survive today with the displacement of millions? Would those millions not blame those in hierarchy or God?

A lone wolf against other wolves and nature typically doesn’t live as long as a subservient wolf in a pack. Even if it does, more often than not the lone wolf must work twice as hard to achieve the same goals, same kill, and same shelter. No one wants to hear that their duty is to follow another, especially if they are dependent on that person.

This is a matter of perspective though: a master-slave relationship. We are so independent in the West that we view ourselves as slaves to one another, but in reality, we are on equal levels in a give-and-take relationship. Those whom we follow give us something we do not have, and perhaps deep down, can’t possess. Instead of looking at hierarchy as subservient, we should view it the same way we view a manufacturer of ice cream and us the consumer.

Hierarchy is found in all things, from the microscopic to the macroscopic – from atoms to galaxy clusters. Yet the Leftist who holds Darwin Scientism as a cornerstone of their individual personality ideology believes that although man is an animal, it is he who is better than the other animals, and thus hierarchy should not exist.

The problem with this is it assumes all brains and bodies are the same – they derive their blank slate beliefs from Locke and the betterment of man from Rousseau. In the aristocratic mindset – as the previous pages have outlined – we know that some people have better suited brains and better suited beliefs and better suited bodies to perform certain tasks. Biology alone creates hierarchy of others, and to disregard this is trans ideology – the belief you are something you are not.

It is insane to NOT assume that there are people who are better to lead than others. It is insane to NOT assume that people would rather be led than lead. These two opposing ideals, one of leadership and one eager to follow, are something as old as time and cross species boundaries from the micro to the macro.
