RELIGION CREATES UNITY AND COHESION

The common man isn't going to read The Myth of Sisyphus and derive meaning in the absurd. They are not going to read the philosophers that academia ascribes as foundational. The common man finds its ethics, morality, sense of justice, meaning, and purpose in stories of the divine. There is no shame in this, and it is perfectly acceptable; needed for all of time in the past and into the future. In this time that I exist we have movies and books detailing superheroes plastered across media that make millions in currency. These characters are larger than life, battling evil, ascribing a moral code, and performing acts bigger than the viewer or reader. But they lack the divine element, a justice above man, and a weave from which society is derived.

As our society becomes more fractured and decentralized, it is easier to determine likeness in the ideas we subscribe to and the morals we adhere to. The word Christian invokes an entire ideal of values, the same for Muslim, and the same for Buddhist; a single word can easily sum up a person. That isn't to say there aren't differences, for all religions and ideals have different camps of beliefs. But two sects of Christians in a holy war are more akin than the different sects of Islam. So, there is the direct unity and cohesion that religion creates, but also entire societies and civilizations built on their ideals.

If your population is 100 Christians and you import 100 Muslims, that creates disunity by fracturing the underlying principles of the original society. This is why Leftism is abhorrent and I consider it anti-civilizational, because this type of demographic replacement is what is happening in Western civilization in my time, in the name of Leftism's ideals. One has to wonder if it is not by design, because it takes a complete reckless abandonment of historical, sociological, psychological, and theological understanding. If compassion is the complete driving force for replacement, then the only answer is to go in the opposite direction to save our civilization; punishing those that forced integration so as to serve as a warning in the future.

So, religion can create unity and cohesion in society while creating the "other" as a societal immune system response. This is normal human animal behavior and we find this in all elements from an anthropological and psychological standpoint when existing with other people. Where it creates unity and cohesion is by giving a society shared experiences, shared values, shared goals, shared afterlife prospects, and shared culture. Regardless of the religion and one's inclination of religion as a whole or of one in particular, this is a fact.

High trust societies are created because of shared values. The common man conforms one's life around morals and ethics based on religion, with philosophy injected at the seams, or through government intervention with the sword as a punitive mechanism, or a mixture of both. Although punishment is a means to an end, it is something as a result of a crime, not an ethical framework why you shouldn't commit a crime. But religion, any religion, can be used as a means of justifying crimes as well when unity is threatened. Any belief structure absent of religion can do the same, such as politics and political philosophy. 

Most belief structures attempt to create unity and cohesion; however, there are some so destructive that the very basis for their existence is to sow destruction and discontent. For those beliefs I will leave the names out because I do not believe we should invoke them and give them legitimacy. Absent of those, unity and cohesion come from the community, and bubble up from there. Knowing that your neighbor shares similar beliefs as you eases a psychological anxiety of the reptilian mind in that your neighbor may not be a threat that you have to contend with. This fact bubbles up from a local community to that of a township, then all the way to a region, country, and beyond. And this is where utopian ideology can take over: "if they just believed the same things as us then a perfect world could be had." That part is addressed elsewhere in this book.
