CIVILIZATIONAL INCENTIVES

In this we find that in societies in which the free market is enacted, the highest-paying jobs are ones in which private equity is injected. Here we find the Information Technology jobs and the support structures of business earn the most money. IT is effectively nothing more than the movement, automation, the input and output, and displaying of data. These positions do nothing for civilization’s progression outside of increasing the wealth of private individuals and companies. Society survived on clay tablet, papyrus, and paper for millennia, and it will one day return.

However, what we find is jobs and careers that support civilization and society earning median and less than a country's median salary. Emergency management technicians, nurses, mental health technicians, water workers, welders, sewage treatment workers, power line operators, educators, road workers, and so many more who earn barely subsistence wages but without whom civilization as we know it would crumble. The free market doesn’t have a variable in the proverbial “value to civilizational existence” salary equation, but it should.

When the aqueduct workers stopped working on the transmission lines for Rome, it hastened society's demise. The average man, with limited knowledge, doesn’t have the necessary understanding of multiple disciplines to ensure water delivery. With the complexity of modernity, the average man could never walk into a water treatment plant and run it successfully. Knowledge is passed through knowledge bearers, and when the jobs dry up, there is a collapse function that takes place, the free market enters, and the wages and incentives increase dramatically. This causes secondary and tertiary effects such as increased water costs and associated expenses to the consumer.

Instead of the collapse function triggering, we can add a “civilizational incentive” to certain civilization-fostering jobs. This can easily be enforced by governments by allowing those workers to not pay taxes if they are below the threshold of two times the median salary of a nation. No government rebates, stimulus checks, or hard equations needed. This calculation is as simple as job verification and wage totaling that can be performed in all tax software; it just requires government approval. The two-times median salary portion excludes high-paying positions that already don’t need incentives.
I am a free-market person, but I am not blind to government intervention if civilization and society are at stake. This is an insurance policy for the present and future of the jobs that are needed for a functional civilization. The litmus test for such jobs and careers can be: if the sun threw a coronal mass ejection (CME) at us and the nation went dark, what jobs are required to sustain a complete die-off?

Those civilizationally necessary jobs would look something like:
    • Farmers and ranchers
    • Utility workers (water, sewage, power, roads)
    • First responders and firefighters
    • Healthcare and support staff
    • Military and State Guards
    • Loggers and miners
    • Logistics
    • Manufacturing (food, textiles, fabrication, candle makers, metalworking, paper milling, oil drilling, pharmaceuticals, petrochemical)
    • Clergy
    • Educators
    
This wouldn’t prevent mass casualties, but it would allow a level of sustainment that those survivors and the generations after could slowly rebuild civilization again. That is why we need to incentivize the civilizational man and protect their knowledge from fading.
