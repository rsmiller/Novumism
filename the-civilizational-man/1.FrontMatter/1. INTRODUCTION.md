INTRODUCTION

This is the second book in a series of books I have published or intend to publish in the future. In my first book, Novum Carta, I created a religion and religious philosophy by synthesizing multiple religious belief systems into one. I created Novum Carta in the darkest time of my life, having lost everything and everyone, when I daily weighed the option to end my existence.

I needed a “great work” to take my mind out of the nihilistic spirals of despair and to move the goalpost of suicide to after my great work was finalized. I knew that projects and goals can offset suicide; instead of ending myself today, put it off to tomorrow. Novum Carta was my attempt to create a means to record my beliefs about religion, to serve as a means to endure some legacy, to give myself meaning and purpose, and to give myself time to heal my mind and reorder my life.

These volume of works are classified as Novumism. Novum directly translates to "new" but can also be interpated as novelty. When creating these works one can choose to follow the religious aspects (Novum Carta), the philosphy (this book) or my metaphysics (The Great Orchistration). I developed a comprehensive religious and philosophical system where you can pick and choose, you don't need to accept the totality.

This book can serve both as a detailed expansion of Novum Carta and as a separate entity. There are clearly chapters in this book that are almost word for word found in Novum Carta. There are clearly concepts here that share the same origin. Just reading this book isn’t losing context. In Novum Carta, I present the ideas presented there in a religious framework, whereas this book is more agnostic and about moral and ethical frameworks centering around duty to civilization.

If you were to summarize a “too long; didn’t read” paragraph regarding what this book is about, it would be this: humanity has survived for hundreds of thousands of years, through cycles of destruction and birth, giving rise to civilizations that rise and fall, and through those times there is a perennial set of rules and laws that allowed humanity to survive.

Writing this book caused me to suffer a depression severe enough that I had to stay in a mental ward for five days. It allowed me time to get off my devices, rest my brain, gain perspective, and meet interesting people. This happened because society is chaos and chaos ruins the mind. There are so many people vying for power, influence, and fighting for some just cause. Prior to the internet, these people handed out leaflets and the media rarely covered them. Now, all groups and individuals with ideologies are front and center on all media feeds at once, and you can’t escape it without becoming “digitally Amish.”

A large part of why I had to go to the ward is because I have a problem, an almost autistic problem. Where others can see primary, secondary, and tertiary effects of a given action, mine extend beyond by orders of magnitude, and at the end of that is a collapse function that is triggered at some point in the timeline for various reasons. 

Kant had a perspective that there is no guarantee that what we think will come tomorrow, based on our experiences and those around us, will come to fruition. He said there is a probability that our previous experience can determine what events may come, but it is not granted. I see a probability matrix when I see cause and effect drawn out over multiple iterations and generational paths. In this probability, I see a collapse function, where the more complex the events, the more probable the collapse. 

For me, I embody this aspect that haunted Kant, and I experience it at a biological level. I think that tomorrow isn’t deterministic based on past experience, but rather a probability that it won't be. Because of this, the events that unfolded in my time stir me deeply, because society is in the collapse function, at the event horizon of a black hole. The question I ask myself is: how can the individual embody a code of ethics that will bubble up in society and keep the whole thing from collapsing what generations before built and died for?

So, the basis for this book is that I see that the current landscape has put Western civilization on a crash course toward a dystopian hellscape, far outweighing the aftereffects of 9/11 and Covid-19. This is me hand-waving and blowing the whistle. This is me showing the problems I see that will escalate and enforce the collapse function. And ultimately, what I see that can be done to escape the collapse function and/or to lessen its effects for future generations.

If you have read my previous book Novum Carta, you may sense a departure from the Middle Way aspect in politics that I define there. However, at the time I viewed Conservatism as a political philosophy of change. In reality, it is a philosophy of stabilization, of prevention, not that of change. So, in my time of writing this, when I see where the political Left is going and has morphed into, Conservatism isn't an opposing force but rather a line in the sand that is always moving further and further left. My ideology isn't a departure from the Middle Way but rather a reframing given new information.

In my time, the Left is migrating to totalitarian Leftism and revolution. This was apparent to me as I experienced the intense culture wars of BLM and the societal destruction wrought by the Left by Covid-19. If we take the totalitarian Right, which is not represented to any degree in my society, and attempt to find the Middle Way between the two, I believe my Realizations and societal laws I present in this book are appropriate. In this framing, I apply Perennialism for civilizational survival, and naturally this falls to the right of the middle.

As I have learned more about the world and gained some wisdom, I no longer believe the concept I present in my past book of Radical Center Politics is something achievable for humanity. I don't believe this is possible because I gave the human animal more cognitive ability than I gave credit for; for if I can't adequately find the borders between two extremes, how can everyone do the same? I do, however, believe a Skillsocracy would be prudent, and it is explored more in the book.
